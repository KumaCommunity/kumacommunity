# Exfil Plan

_Exfil_ is short for _exfiltration_. In this guide, it means an evacuation plan:
how to leave one location and reach another when staying put is unsafe or
impractical.

The plan should answer three basic questions: when to leave, where to go, and
how to get there. Follow current evacuation orders and directions from local
authorities even if they differ from your written plan.

## Getting Started

1. Identify the location you might need to leave.
2. Choose one or more destinations where you could stay safely.
3. Decide whether you can reach each destination in a single trip.
   - If not, identify possible stops or overnight locations along the way.

Use specific addresses, coordinates, or unmistakable landmarks so everyone
involved knows the destination. Confirm that the destination is available and
appropriate for the people and animals traveling with you.

## Define Your Trigger

Before working through the logistics, decide what should prompt departure.

- Which conditions would make the current location unsafe?
- Are there official evacuation instructions or warnings?
- Who makes the decision if the group needs to leave?
- How much time might be available to pack and depart?
- What would make you leave earlier than planned?

These triggers help you prepare. **Do not delay an official evacuation order or
stay in immediate danger because a personal threshold has not been reached.**

## Fill in the Details

The details will depend on the situation: wildfire, hurricane, flooding,
infrastructure outage, or another disruption.

### Transportation

- Will you drive, walk, use public transit, or arrange a ride?
- Who is responsible for each vehicle?
- Can everyone travel together, including people with mobility needs and pets?
- What is the backup if a vehicle becomes unavailable?

### Route Planning

Identify a primary route and reasonable alternatives before an emergency.

- What bridges, intersections, low-water crossings, or other hazards could
  affect travel?
- Are fuel, charging, and rest stops available along the way?
- Could weather, closures, or an evacuation order change which route is safe?
- Is there a destination you can use if the first one becomes unavailable?

Review routes on a map and, where practical, travel them during normal
conditions. You do not need to drive every alternative route in advance.

During an evacuation, **follow official routing and road closures**. Never drive
around barricades or through floodwater. A mapped route is not a guarantee that
the road is passable.

### Navigation

- Will your navigation method work without cellular service?
- Have you downloaded offline maps for the area?
- Would a current paper map be a useful backup?
- Does everyone who may drive know the destination?

Do not assume an online map will have current closure information when
connectivity is unreliable. Check official advisories whenever they are
available.

### Resources & Sustainment

- Do you need fuel or charging stops?
- Can you reach the destination without an overnight stay?
- Will you need a hotel, shelter, or another place to rest?
- Can you pay without an internet connection or working card terminal?
- Do you need cash, food, water, prescription medication, or medical equipment?
- Do children, older adults, pets, or people with disabilities need additional
  supplies or arrangements?

### Documentation

Keep essential information accessible, with a backup where practical:

- Government identification
- Insurance and property information
- Emergency contacts
- Medical information and medication lists
- Pet identification and vaccination records, when relevant

Protect sensitive documents from loss or unnecessary disclosure.

### Communications Plan

Decide how you will contact each other during travel.

- Who needs an update when you leave and arrive?
- What times or milestones will you use for check-ins?
- What happens if someone misses a check-in?
- What communication method will you try if cellular service is unavailable?
- Where will separated travelers meet if contact cannot be restored?

A scheduled check-in is useful, but it is not a separate communication method.
Use the options documented in your **PACE Plan**, including a prearranged
meeting location when appropriate.

## Build the Checklist

Turn the decisions above into a packing and departure checklist.

Decide:

- What stays in your go bag?
- What remains in the vehicle?
- What is staged at home?
- What must be packed immediately before leaving, such as medication or pet supplies?
- Which items could be obtained along the route, and what happens if they are unavailable?

Prioritize essential items and what your transportation can safely carry. Avoid
packing so much that it delays departure.

## Prepare

### Stage Supplies

- Gather essential supplies and put them where they can be found quickly.
- Set a realistic budget for anything you still need.
- Buy necessities first rather than trying to complete the entire list at once.

### Coordinate

- Review the plan with everyone involved.
- Confirm roles, destinations, and contact information.
- Make sure each person understands when the plan might change.

### Rehearse

- Review routes and destinations together.
- Try offline navigation or a paper map during an ordinary trip.
- Estimate travel time under normal conditions, recognizing that an evacuation
  may take much longer.
- Practice the departure checklist without creating an unnecessary emergency response.

### Reduce Friction

- Keep vehicles maintained and reasonably fueled or charged.
- Keep essential documents and supplies organized.
- Check that transport arrangements work for everyone traveling.

## Act

When the plan is activated:

1. Confirm the latest official instructions and the safety of your intended destination.
2. Account for everyone traveling and collect essential supplies.
3. Let your designated contacts know where you intend to go when it is safe and
   possible to do so.
4. Use the safest available route and adjust if conditions change.
5. Check in when you arrive.

Leave earlier, change destinations, or abandon a planned route if conditions
require it. Avoid unnecessary stops, but do not sacrifice safety to keep a
schedule.

## After Action Review

After a drill or evacuation, ask:

- What worked?
- What failed or caused delays?
- Which assumptions turned out to be wrong?
- Did the destination, route, and check-ins work as expected?

Update the plan while the details are still fresh.
