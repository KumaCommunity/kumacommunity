# Safe Firearm Storage

Firearms should be secured against unauthorized access whenever they are not
under an authorized person's direct control. The right storage method depends on
whether a firearm is kept for ordinary use, long-term storage, or immediate
defensive access.

**For general storage, keep firearms unloaded and locked, with ammunition
secured separately.** A firearm specifically designated for personal or home
defense is the only type you should consider keeping loaded while stored. Even
then, it needs a locked container that prevents unauthorized access.

## Core Principles

### 1. Prevent Unauthorized Access

Firearms must not be accessible to:

- Children
- Guests and visitors who are not authorized to handle them
- Untrained or unauthorized individuals
- Anyone who should not have access because of a current safety concern

If someone can handle a stored firearm without your knowledge or permission,
improve the storage arrangement. Hiding a firearm or placing it out of reach is
not a substitute for a lock.

### 2. Maintain Authorized Access

Access requirements are different for general storage and defensive storage.

- **General and long-term storage:** Prioritize preventing unauthorized handling
  and theft. Immediate access is not a requirement.
- **Designated defensive storage:** Consider whether an authorized adult can
  access the secured firearm when needed, without leaving it accessible to
  anyone else.

Do not make every firearm in the home quickly accessible merely because one has
a defensive purpose.

### 3. Use Physical Security and Household Rules

Physical security options include:

- Locking gun safes
- Lockboxes and dedicated quick-access safes
- Cable or chamber locks as additional safeguards
- Locked storage areas, when paired with suitable firearm-specific security

Support those measures with:

- Clear household rules
- Training for authorized users
- Protected keys, combinations, and access credentials
- Periodic checks of locks and storage equipment

A locked room by itself may not provide the same protection as a properly
secured firearm container. Choose measures appropriate to the risk and people
who may enter the space.

## Home Storage

### General and Long-Term Storage

Firearms not specifically designated for immediate defensive use should be
stored **unloaded**.

- Verify the firearm is unloaded using its manufacturer's procedure.
- Secure it in a locked safe or suitable lockbox.
- Store ammunition separately in a locked location.
- Limit access to authorized individuals.

A full-size safe can hold several firearms and may offer better protection than
a lightweight cabinet, but security depends on its construction, lock,
anchoring, and installation. Smaller safes or lockboxes can be appropriate where
space is limited or separate secure locations are needed.

### Defensive Storage

A firearm kept specifically for personal or home defense has different
accessibility requirements. **This is the only category of firearm you should
consider keeping loaded while in storage.** Unloaded, locked storage remains the
safest general recommendation. Keeping a defensive firearm loaded introduces
additional risk that must be addressed through secure storage and household
precautions.

If you choose to store a designated defensive firearm loaded:

- Use a dedicated, **locked** quick-access safe or lockbox designed for firearm
  storage.
- Keep keys, codes, and other access methods away from children and unauthorized
  individuals.
- Do not rely on a high shelf, concealment, or a hidden location instead of a
  locking device.
- Choose an opening method that authorized users can operate reliably.
- Test the lock as directed by its manufacturer and maintain batteries where applicable.
- Consider the people who live in or visit the home when selecting the container
  and its location.

Mechanical push-button, electronic keypad, and biometric safes each have
advantages and limitations. Reliability varies by model, construction, battery
condition, and maintenance. Do not assume one lock type is always more
dependable than another.

Practice opening the **empty** storage container and familiarize yourself with
its operation without handling a loaded firearm unnecessarily. Any firearm
handling should follow the basic safety rules and manufacturer's instructions.

## Children and Guests

Children may be curious, and visitors may not know the household's rules. The
storage plan should not depend on others recognizing a firearm or choosing not
to touch it.

If children live in or visit the home:

- Keep all firearms secured in locked storage when not under direct authorized control.
- Do not share combinations or access credentials.
- Keep keys and backup keys secured.
- Check that the storage device cannot be opened or removed by an unauthorized person.

If guests are staying overnight or other people are present, review whether your
existing storage remains appropriate. Increase security when needed instead of
relying solely on verbal rules.

If someone in the household is at risk of harming themselves or others, consider
temporary storage that prevents their access, following applicable law and
guidance from a qualified professional where appropriate.

## Vehicle Storage

A locked vehicle is not, by itself, secure firearm storage. Vehicle break-ins
and firearm theft are significant risks, so avoid leaving a firearm in a vehicle
whenever possible.

If temporary vehicle storage is necessary and lawful:

- Use a dedicated locked firearm container.
- Secure the container against easy removal when the vehicle and product allow it.
- Place it out of plain sight, while recognizing that concealment does not
  replace a lock.
- Follow applicable firearm transport and vehicle-storage laws.
- Remove the firearm as soon as practical and return it to appropriate storage
  at home or another secure location.

A standard glovebox or center console, even one with a basic lock, should not
automatically be treated as a secure firearm container. Evaluate the actual
construction and locking arrangement.

Do not leave a firearm in a vehicle overnight as a routine storage method. When
returning home, move it into the appropriate secured storage rather than leaving
it in the car.

## Balance Access and Security

Review your storage choices when household circumstances change.

Consider:

- Who lives in the home
- Whether children or guests visit regularly
- Who is authorized and trained to access the firearms
- Which, if any, firearm is specifically designated for defensive use
- What storage laws apply where you live or travel
- Whether the present arrangement still prevents unauthorized access

The same safe or lockbox may not be the right choice for every firearm or every
household.

## Maintenance

Storage devices require periodic checks just like other equipment.

Verify that:

- Locks, hinges, and mounting hardware function properly.
- Keys and backup access methods are still available to authorized users and
  secured from everyone else.
- Batteries are replaced or charged according to the manufacturer's instructions.
- Electronic or biometric access works consistently.
- Moisture, humidity, and corrosion are being managed appropriately.
- Firearms remain secured and in the expected storage condition.

If a lock or storage container is unreliable, repair or replace it before
relying on it again. Handle firearms safely during inspections, and avoid
unnecessary handling of loaded firearms.
