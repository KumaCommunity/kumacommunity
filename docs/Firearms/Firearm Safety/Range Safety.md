# Range Safety

Safe range habits are straightforward, but they require consistent attention.
These rules apply every time you handle a firearm, regardless of experience.

Follow the range's posted rules and the instructions of its range safety
officers. They may have additional requirements for the facility or activity.

## The Four Pillars

### 1. Always Keep the Muzzle Pointed in a Safe Direction

Never point a firearm at anything you do not intend to shoot.

At a conventional shooting range, the safe direction is generally downrange
toward the designated backstop. In the field or other settings, you also need to
consider:

- How far a projectile can travel
- What lies beyond the target
- Whether the backstop can stop the ammunition
- Hard surfaces that could cause a ricochet

Maintain control of the muzzle even when moving, stumbling, or handling a
firearm you have verified is unloaded.

### 2. Keep Your Finger Off the Trigger Until You Are Ready to Shoot

Keep your trigger finger straight and outside the trigger guard until your
sights are on target and you have decided to fire.

This matters while:

- Drawing or presenting a firearm
- Loading and unloading
- Clearing malfunctions
- Changing position or adjusting your stance

Do not rely on a mechanical safety to replace trigger discipline.

### 3. Treat Every Firearm as If It Is Loaded

Whenever you pick up or receive a firearm, maintain safe muzzle direction and
verify its condition yourself. Do not rely solely on someone else's statement
that it is unloaded.

When checking a firearm according to its manual:

- Remove the magazine or other ammunition source, if applicable.
- Open the action.
- Check that the chamber and feeding path are empty, visually and physically
  when safe and appropriate for the design.

A verified-clear firearm still requires safe handling.

### 4. Be Sure of Your Target and What Is Beyond It

Before firing, identify:

- The intended target
- What is immediately behind it
- Where a missed shot or a round that passes through could travel

If you cannot clearly identify the target or confirm an appropriate backstop, do
not shoot.

## Additional Range Safety Practices

### Unload Your Firearm When Not Actively Shooting

At the range, load only when permitted and when you are preparing to shoot.

When finished firing, follow the firearm's manual and range procedures to:

- Remove the magazine or other ammunition source.
- Open the action and clear the chamber.
- Leave the action open when required.
- Insert a chamber flag into the open action or chamber when required by the range.

Transport firearms unloaded and secured in accordance with applicable laws and
range requirements.

### Follow Cease-Fire Procedures

When a cease-fire is called:

- Stop firing immediately.
- Keep the muzzle pointed downrange.
- Unload and make the firearm safe as instructed.
- Step away from the firing line when directed.
- Do not handle firearms or ammunition while people are downrange or while the
  range prohibits handling.

Wait for the range officer or other authorized person to declare that firing may
resume. If you notice an unsafe condition, call attention to it immediately.

### Do Not Rely on Mechanical Safeties

Mechanical safeties can help prevent unintended firing, but they can fail or be
used incorrectly.

An unloaded firearm with its action open and chamber verified clear is the
normal condition for many range cease-fires. That does not eliminate the need
for muzzle and trigger discipline.

### Use the Correct Ammunition

Use only ammunition appropriate for the firearm's chambering and permitted by
the manufacturer.

Check the markings on both the firearm and ammunition. Similar-looking
cartridges may not be interchangeable, and incorrect ammunition can cause a
dangerous malfunction.

### If the Firearm Fails to Fire

If you press the trigger and the firearm does not fire:

1. Keep the muzzle pointed downrange and keep your finger away from the trigger.
2. Keep your face away from the action or breech.
3. Wait **at least 30 seconds** in case of delayed ignition, or longer if the
   firearm manufacturer's instructions or range rules require it. Some firearm
   types, including muzzleloaders, require different procedures.
4. Notify the range safety officer if one is present, and follow the firearm's
   instructions for safely unloading or clearing the malfunction.

Do not immediately open the action after a suspected misfire. A failure to fire
does not mean the cartridge is harmless.

### Wear Eye and Ear Protection

Wear appropriate eye and hearing protection whenever shooting or present on an
active range.

- Hearing damage can be permanent. Indoor ranges may warrant both earplugs and earmuffs.
- Eye protection helps guard against debris, ejected cases, and
  malfunction-related hazards.
- Wash your hands after shooting or handling ammunition, particularly before
  eating or drinking.

### Check for Barrel Obstructions

Before loading, make sure the bore is unobstructed using the firearm
manufacturer's safe inspection procedure.

If a shot produces an unusually weak report, reduced recoil, or another abnormal
result, **stop firing immediately**. This may indicate a squib load, in which a
projectile has become lodged in the barrel. Do not fire another round until the
firearm has been safely cleared and the bore confirmed unobstructed. Ask a range
officer or qualified gunsmith for help if necessary.

Never inspect a loaded firearm by looking into its muzzle.

### Do Not Modify Safety Mechanisms Without Proper Training

Improper changes to a trigger, safety, or internal components can make a firearm
unsafe.

If a mechanism does not work as intended, stop using the firearm and consult the
manufacturer or a qualified gunsmith.

### Learn the Firearm You Are Using

Firearms differ in their controls and handling procedures. Before using one, learn:

- How to load and unload it
- How to verify that it is clear
- How to open and secure the action
- How the safety works, if equipped
- What its manual says about malfunctions and maintenance

If you are unfamiliar with a firearm, ask a qualified instructor for help before
using it.

## Range Conduct

Clear instructions and consistent habits make it easier for everyone to identify
and correct problems. If you are unsure about a rule or a firearm's condition,
stop handling it and ask the range safety officer.
